All About Monarch Butterflies

The Monarch Butterfly is famous for their recognizable wing patterns and lengty migrations across North America. Every year, millions of these bright orange and black butterflies fly thousands of kilometers to reach their wintering grounds in the forests of Mexico. What makes this migration truly phenomenal is that not a single butterfly makes the entire round trip. Instead, it takes several generations to complete the cycle, with the "super generation" living much longer than usual just to survive the long flight South.

SPECIES: Monarch Butterfly

SCIENTIFIC NAME: Danaus plexippus

MIGRATION DISTANCE: Up to 4,800 km

DESTINATION: Oyamel fir forests in Mexico (Winter)

GENERATIONS: 4 generations to complete the full cycle

DIET: Milkweed (as caterpillars) and Nectar (as adults)

KEY FEATURE: Bright orange and black warning colors
