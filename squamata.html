All About Squamata

Squamata is the largest recent order of reptiles, making up the clade that includes all lizards and snakes. The name 'squamata' comes from Latin origin meaning "scaled," which makes sense because these animals are known for their protective, scaly skin that they periodically shed as they grow. Squamates are incredibly diverse and can be found in almost every environment, from the driest deserts to the highest rainforest canopies. Squamates share unique jaw structures that help become highly dangerous predators, and to digest prey with ease.

ORDER NAME: Squamata

COMMON NAME: Scaled Reptiles

INCLUDES: Lizards, Snakes, and Amphisbaenians

SKIN TYPE: Horny scales or shields

UNIQUE TRAIT: Movable quadrate bones (helps open jaws wide)

SPECIES COUNT: Over 10,000 distinct species

KEY FEATURE: Periodic shedding of skin (ecdysis)
