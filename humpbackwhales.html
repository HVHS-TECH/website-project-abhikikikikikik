All About Humpback Whales

Humpback Whales are incredible world travelers of the ocean. They have one of the longest migration paths of any mammal, and swim thousands of kilometers between their feeding grounds near the poles and their warm-tropical breeding waters. During the month long journies, they usually don't eat at all, living off the thick layer of blubber they stored up during the summer. These migrations are essential for their calves, who need the calm, warm water to grow strong before heading back to the icy feeding grounds.

SPECIES: Humpback Whale

SCIENTIFIC NAME: Megaptera novaeangliae

MIGRATION DISTANCE: Up to 25,000 km per year

DESTINATION: Tropical waters (breeding) Polar waters (feeding)

TRAVEL SPEED: 5 to 15 km per hour

SURVIVAL FACT: They live off blubber and barely eat during the journey

KEY FEATURE: Massive pectoral fins and breaching behavior
