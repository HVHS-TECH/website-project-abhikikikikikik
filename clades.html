Understanding Clades

In biology, a clade is a group of organisms that includes a single common ancestor and all of its descendants. Think of it like a "branch" on the tree of life. Scientists use clades to show how different species are related to each other based on their evolutionary history rather than just looking at how they appear on the outside.

The Tree of Life

Every time a new trait evolves—like feathers, four limbs, or even specialized teeth—a new clade is formed. These groups, like Sauropsids (reptiles and birds) or Synapsids (mammals), help us track how life has changed over millions of years. By studying these branches, we can see that even though a bird and a crocodile look different, they actually share a very close evolutionary branch!
