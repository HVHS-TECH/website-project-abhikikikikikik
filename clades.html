Understanding Clades

A clade is a group of organisms that had a single common ancestor and all of its descendants. You can think of it like a "branch" on the tree of life. Scientists and researchers use clades to show how different species are related to each other based on their evolutionary history rather than just looking at how they appear on the outside.

The Tree of Life

Every time an animal develops and evolves things like feathers, four limbs, or even specialized teeth, a new clade is formed. These groups, like Sauropsids (reptiles and birds) or Synapsids (mammals), help researchers track how life has changed over millions of years. By studying these branches, we can see that even though a bird and a crocodile look different, they actually share a very close evolutionary branch.
