All About Polar Climates

Polar climates are some of the coldest places on Earth, with the icy Arctic in the north and the frozen continent of Antarctica in the south. Polar climates are covered in snow and ice for most of the year, with temperatures rarely rising above freezing. Despite the harsh and cold conditions, the surrounding oceans are rich with life, containing massive mammals like blue whales and humpback whales. On land, animals need thick fur, layers of blubber, or unique hibernation patterns—like the wood frog—to survive the long, dark winters where the sun sometimes doesn't rise for half the year.

REGION TYPE: Polar Climate (Ice Cap / Tundra)

AVERAGE TEMP: Usually below 0°C (Can reach -80°C)

PRECIPITATION: Very low (Considered a "Cold Desert")

DAYLIGHT: 24-hour sun in summer, 24-hour dark in winter

NATIVE ANIMALS: Polar Bears, Penguins, Arctic Foxes

KEY FEATURE: Permanent layers of ice and permafrost

LOCATIONS: Antarctica and the Arctic Circle
